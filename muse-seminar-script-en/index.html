01 The Basics of AI (Slides 5–7, about 13 min)

〔This part has one key idea: AI changed from "answering" to "working."〕

Slide 5Four years (about 7 min)

In the last few years, AI changed very fast. I will not talk about technology. We only look at one thing: what AI can do. Four years.

2023: AI learned to talk.

ChatGPT came out at the end of 2022. In 2023, it spread very fast, all over the world. In spring, a smarter new version, GPT-4, also came out. For the first time, AI could talk with us like a person. You write, it answers. It could write emails, fix your sentences, even make small stories. Everyone was surprised. "AI can talk like this?" This was the year generative AI spread to everyone.

Did anyone here use ChatGPT? … I see, some of you. Good — today's talk will be easy for you. And if you never used it, no problem at all. We start from zero, together.

2024: AI learned to see, and to think.

The next year, AI got two new powers. One is "seeing." Show it a photo, and it understands the photo. It can also read the words in the photo. It also learned to talk with a voice. The second power is "thinking." For a hard question, it does not answer at once. It thinks step by step, and then answers.

So: the AI of 2023 was "a person who talks well." In 2024, it became "a person who can see, and can think."

2025: AI started to work. (This is short — details come in the second half.)

Answering was not enough. AI started to make finished things and hand them to us. Programmers used it first. The AI wrote programs and gave them the finished work. AI changed from "a thing that answers" to "a thing that gives results."

2026: AI came to our side. (Short, too.)

This year. The working AI became a phone app. It is not only for experts now. It is the star of tonight.

Let me put these four years into one sentence. Please remember this one: The first years were "AI that answers." Now, it is "AI that works."

The first years: answering. Now: working. In the second half, we talk about "working."

Slide 6How generative AI behaves (about 3.5 min)

Before we use it, please learn two habits of AI. It takes one minute, and it will save you from mistakes.

Think of generative AI as an assistant who read a huge number of books. It read so much, so when you ask something, it almost always has an answer. But please remember two things.

One: AI is not a search engine. It does not find "the one right answer" on the internet. It builds sentences from what it read. So its answers are useful — but do not believe everything.

Two: it makes mistakes with full confidence. It does not often say "I don't know." Sometimes it makes a mistake that sounds very true. So for important things — money, dates, addresses — please check one time with your own eyes at the end. This habit comes back in the safety talk, in the second half.

Slide 7Why people stop using it (about 2.5 min)

Some people here tried AI before. It was fun at first. But then they stopped opening the app. Why? Usually, three reasons.

One: ask, and finish. You see the answer, and that is the end.

Two: the AI does not know you. Every time, you must explain everything from the start.

Three: the AI only "talks." It does not "do." It writes a plan for you, but you still do the searching and the booking yourself.

These three reasons become two problems: "how to ask" and "how to let it work." First, we fix "how to ask." That is the next part.

02 The Pattern for Asking (Slides 8–13, about 14 min)

〔This part has one key idea: first the background, then the result you want.〕

Slide 8The official four (about 2 min)

When we learn a language, we first learn patterns, right? With a pattern, you change the words, and you can make many new sentences. Asking AI has a pattern, too. Learn this one pattern, and you do not need to remember many tricks.

And one thing, so you can feel safe: I did not make this pattern. OpenAI — the company that made ChatGPT — and Anthropic, another very big AI company, both write the same four things in their official guides: the goal, the background, the shape you want, and the boundaries — the things the AI must not do, and the things it must ask you about first. The two biggest companies say the same thing.

Today, we make these four into two moves and one safety line. Here is the answer first: First, tell the background. Next, say the result you want. Last, add: "If you are not sure, ask me first." Let's go one by one.

Slide 9The main sentence (about 0.5 min)

〔This slide is a dark blue screen. Speak slowly, with pauses.〕

These four things become one easy sentence. Like this:

Make the background clear. Make the result clear. Let the AI do the "how."

I will explain the reasons one by one. And at the end, we come back to this sentence.

Slide 10First, tell the background (about 4 min)

The AI does not know you. It does not know where you live, how many people are in your family, or what you like. So before you ask, first tell the background. It is the same as a new person at work. First you say who you are. Then you say the job.

These are not my words. In Anthropic's official guide, it says: think of the AI as "a smart new worker who knows nothing about your life." The more you tell it about your situation, the better its work.

The background has about three things: who you are, your situation, and what you have in your hands.

An example. "Think of a weekend plan for me." — This has no background. Now let's change it: "I live in Osaka. We are two people, me and my mother. She is 70 years old. We go out on Saturday afternoon. A plan with not much walking is good." — This is background. The same request — but the answer changes completely.

The official guide gives us one more good check. I give it to you: after you write, read your words one time. If a person who does not know you cannot understand it, the AI cannot understand it, too. You do not need to remember rules. Please remember only this check. 〔Source: Anthropic official guide, "Prompting best practices"〕

Slide 11Say the result (about 4 min)

After the background, do not make a list of steps — "first do this, then do that." Decide only one thing: the final result you want, and its shape.

In OpenAI's official guide, it says: "Start from the result. Do not list the steps." If you let the AI choose the "how," it will search, compare, and fix things by itself. 〔Source: OpenAI official guide, "Prompting"〕

For example, say this: "Please give me three plans. For each plan, write the place, how to get there, and about how much it costs." The result and the shape are clear. What to search, and how to put it in order — the AI thinks about that. Its order is often better than our order.

One more small tip, also from the guides: say what you want, not what you don't want. Not "don't make it long," but "in 200 words or less." Not "don't be formal," but "like a message to a friend." A clear goal works better than a "no." 〔Source: Anthropic official guide〕

Now, back to our one sentence. This is the most important sentence today. Make the background clear. Make the result clear. Let the AI do the "how."

Make the background clear. Make the result clear. Let the AI do the "how."

〔間〕

Now, two safety lines to add. One: for an important request, add this sentence: "If you are not sure, do not decide alone. Ask me first." OpenAI calls this a "boundary" — a line for what the AI must ask about. You do not need many boundaries. One or two important ones are enough. 〔Source: OpenAI official guide〕 This one sentence is very powerful in the second half, when we give the AI bigger work.

Two: if you want a special style or shape, show one example. It is the surest way. Anthropic's guide also says examples are one of the best ways to guide the AI. One example is better than ten explanations. 〔Source: Anthropic official guide〕

Slide 12Let's compare (about 3 min)

Please look at the screen. We asked the same thing in two ways.

On the left is the "idea of the moment" way: "Think of a weekend plan for me." The answer was a list of famous places in Osaka. It was not wrong — but we cannot use it as it is.

On the right is today's pattern. We told the background — two people, with my mother, not much walking — and the result we want: three plans, with how to get there and the cost. The answer was a plan we can choose and use right away.

The difference is not the AI. The difference is how we ask. With the right way to ask, a normal AI gives you good work.

And you do not start from zero — you only say the part you want to change. In the second half, you will see this with your own eyes. 〔Source: OpenAI official guide〕

03 What Is an Agent? (Slides 16–18, about 11 min)

〔This part has one key idea: an agent is "an AI that works by itself."〕

Slide 16Chat AI and Agent (about 5 min)

In the first half, I said AI is now at the "working" stage. This "working AI" has a name: Agent. This is word four from Slide 4 — I only gave you the name there.

The difference is very simple. Please look at this picture only.

A chat AI is only a "mouth." You ask, it answers. It answers, and it ends.

An agent has "hands." You give it a job, and it searches by itself, does the work by itself, and comes back and says, "It's done."

Think of two new people at work. One person only answers questions. The other person takes a job, makes a plan by himself, does it, and brings you the result. An agent is the second person.

〔Stay on this slide, and continue (about 2 min)〕

This is the next part of the first-half story. In 2025, the "working AI" came. But it lived inside the computers of experts. The screen was black, full of small letters. Normal people saw it and wanted to close it.

This year, the change is a move. It moved from the experts' computers into a phone app. Open it, and you can use it. No hard setup.

So today, we do not learn "one more chat app." We learn a helper who lives in your phone.

Slide 17Why Muse today? (about 3 min)

Products like this are growing. Today, we use Muse. Four reasons, all practical.

One: the shape is right. It is made as a phone app. Open it, and you can use it.

Two: you can start for free. You do not need to pay first. Today's practice fits inside the free plan.

Three: it is from the company with the biggest front door. The maker is Meta — the company of Facebook and Instagram. They made products for normal people for a long time.

Four: it has a watching system. Before it acts outside for you, an independent safety check stops and looks. This is the next part of our talk.

〔Types, in one line (about 1 min) — stay on this slide. If time is short, you can skip this part.〕

Agents in the world are about three types. Big tools that companies give to experts. Ones you build by yourself. And the "carry type" — you put the app on your phone, and you can use it. For a first step, the third type is the safest. Muse is the third type. Now, let's start.

Slide 18Two new doors in Japan (about 3 min)

〔这一页只介绍，不做设定。邮箱地址是自愿取得，今天不弄也行，之后随时可以〕

Muse is not only text chat. In Japan, two new doors opened not long ago. They are also coming to free plans, little by little.

Door one: an email address. You can have an email address only for Muse. Send a long email to it, and Muse makes a short summary, or writes a draft reply for you. You can also give the address to a shop or a company, and Muse receives the answer for you.

Door two: your voice. Tap the phone button, and you can talk with Muse by voice. No typing. It is good when your hands are busy. Some people ask Muse to say "good morning" with a voice message in the morning, too.

Asking is not only by text. You can ask by email, and by voice, too.

Before Practice: Three Settings and Safety (Slides 19–20, about 11 min)

Slide 19Only three settings (about 6 min)

Before we use our hands, we take a few minutes to make Muse "your" assistant. Only three settings. After each one, I will wait for everyone.

One: check your login. Please open the app. If you see the chat screen, you are logged in. If not, please raise your hand. Staff will come to you.

〔間〕

Two: give it a name, and say one sentence about you. With a name, it is not a public window. It is your partner. Then, say one sentence about yourself: who you are, and what you want help with first. For example: "I live in Osaka. First, please help me with weekend plans." Not much. One thing is enough, to start.

〔間〕

Three: decide one rule. Please send this sentence, exactly like this: "If something costs money, or you send a message to someone outside, always ask me first." This rule will protect you from today on. When you send it, please give me a small nod.

Thank you. Three settings, done. Now you have an assistant who knows you.

Slide 20Three safety rules (about 5 min)

Before we let it work, a few minutes about safety. Please listen well here.

Muse has a "guard at the gate." Its name is Sentinel. This is what it means: before Muse sends a message outside for you, or does something with your account, an independent check always stops at the gate and looks. Your real passwords and card information are in a safe that even the AI cannot see.

But I will be honest with you: with a guard, I still cannot say "100% safe." So I ask you for three rules, on your side. Please check them with me on your phone.

Rule one: for permissions, choose "ask me every time." For money, addresses, and messages to outside, the AI must ask you each time. Do not choose "always allow." For example, if you ask the AI to help you sell something, and this check is off, there is a risk: the AI may sell it too cheap by itself, or send your address to the buyer. This one setting stops that.

Rule two: today, we do not connect accounts. In the future, Muse can connect to your email and calendar. But today, we only look. The rule is: one by one. First, "look only." When you get used to it, we open "do it for me," little by little.

Rule three: once a week, look at the record. Open your face mark — your profile. There is a page called Activity. Everything the AI did is there. Look at it quickly, with a cup of tea. That is enough.

That is all — three rules. Please just remember them. Do not be nervous. We start with small things, and give bigger things little by little.
